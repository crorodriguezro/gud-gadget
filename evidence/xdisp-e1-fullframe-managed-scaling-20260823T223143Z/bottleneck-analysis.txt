The dominant bottleneck at the largest safe payload is RAW USB bulk transfer.
At 3,686,400 bytes, the matched managed run measured 100.589 ms p50 logical
host total and 91.058 ms p50 bulk wait; Pi measured 100 ms p50 receive and
4 ms p50 processing. The source-ready-to-worker-end E2E proxy was 119.826 ms
p50, 133.736 ms p95, 160.031 ms max.

The wire rate was 36.864 MB/s p50 decimal (about 35.16 MiB/s on Pi telemetry),
which is consistent across the half and full payloads. One full frame therefore
consumes about 100 ms of transport time, making 20 fps impossible for this RAW
path without reducing bytes or changing the transport boundary. FunctionFS
chunk scheduling is not independently exposed, but 225 internal chunks were
completed inside the exact logical transfer with no failures; no evidence shows
chunk scheduling dominating the measured wire interval.

Point of diminishing returns: reducing admissions from two to one did not raise
presented FPS (both managed runs were 9 fps), because the USB byte rate, not
the remaining admission count, controls the frame interval. The next diagnostic
is TEST LARGE-FRAME LZ4 with the same one-admission geometry, not an admission
redesign in this task.
